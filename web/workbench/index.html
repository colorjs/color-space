color-space Workbench

Workbench

Four directions for the color-space.io front page, built on one shared catalog. Atlas is the one carried forward: its header is decided (the Drawer), and its hero is now a workshop of nine variants to choose between; the live atlas stays as it is.

Directions

  • Atlas

    Catalog-first, editorial – carried forward

    The families as the page itself: each opens with its featured spaces at full reading, the rest follow as compact sliders, and the section titles are the navigation. The live dossier docks beside it; the Drawer header pins over it, and its hero comes in nine variants, below.

  • Guide

    Learning-first, then the atlas by purpose

    Opens on what a color space is, shown on the drifting color in three spaces at once; then the tour as a numbered track, the ten tasks as ways in, a camera-log LUT and the code – and the atlas shelved by purpose, its titles one sticky row.

  • Studio

    Tool-first, with a persistent inspector

    A working surface: a toolbar and filter cells over a dense catalog whose family headings switch family, beside an inspector that always holds something – the current color read across the featured spaces, or the space you open, with its LUT, code, share and agents.

  • Search

    Search-first, one input

    The wordmark, one sentence and one input for a color or a space. A color returns its passport across the spaces; a name or a task returns the spaces that match, the best one opened up.

Atlas heroes

  • PaletteThe promise and one box: a color, a space or a task – the box, the masthead's search and ⌘K open a command palette over the dimmed page.
  • SpectrumA paper band of type between two fields – the same hues as OKLCH draws them above, as HSL draws them below.
  • StepsSame two colors, four paths: the current color to its opposite through sRGB, HSL, CIELAB and OKLCH, stepped.
  • RingsA poster on a visible grid: HSL, CIELAB and OKLCH hue rings, segmented – uneven brightness against even.
  • LineageThe history as a river: every space a band from its year, family by family, 1860 to now.
  • LocusThe CIE 1931 diagram: every chromaticity the eye sees, the sRGB, P3 and Rec. 2020 triangles, the current color in it.
  • ConicA hue circle drawn in fine lines, perceptually even – and the same sweep in HSL, to compare.
  • SpecimenOne color written in a dozen spaces, a type specimen of notations – each one copies.
  • TableThe periodic table of color spaces: every space a tile, grouped by family, each opening its dossier.
